Before you start

Playing on your phone

Controls

  • The on-screen D-pad moves; you can also tap a tile to walk there, or tap a person, sign or menu item to use it.
  • A talks and confirms. B cancels; hold B to run.
  • START opens the menu (Pokédex, party, bag, SAVE). SELECT reorders items and moves.
  • On a computer: arrows/WASD, Z for A, X for B, Enter for START. Gamepads work too.

Keep your save safe

  • Saves live in the phone's browser for that exact address. Playing at your Mac's Wi-Fi address and later on Vercel counts as two different places.
  • Back up from the title screen: SAVE TRANSFER → DOWNLOAD BACKUP. Restore anywhere with LOAD BACKUP FILE.
  • Over plain Wi-Fi (http), COPY SAVE LINK may not be able to reach the clipboard. The download always works.
  • Clearing Safari's website data, or a private tab, wipes the save.

Which starter?

Bulbasaur is the easy start: it beats Brock and Misty and resists Lt. Surge. Squirtle is nearly as smooth and learns Surf-quality attacks early. Charmander is hard mode for the first two gyms: Rock resists Ember and Misty's Water is strong against it. Train a second Pokémon early to share the load (a Nidoran♂ from Route 22, or a Butterfree from Viridian Forest). It pays off later as Charizard.

Your rival always picks the starter that beats yours, so his team changes with your choice (see Rival).

Story order

Walkthrough

Each chapter ends at a badge. The level on the right is a comfortable target for your lead Pokémon before that chapter's boss.

01

Pallet Town to Pewter City

Lv 12–14
  1. Pick your starter in Oak's lab. Your rival battles you straight away (Lv 5).
  2. Walk to Viridian City's Poké Mart, collect Oak's Parcel and bring it back: you get the Pokédex and Poké Balls.
  3. Back in Viridian, the old man now shows you how to catch Pokémon. Remember him for the MissingNo. glitch.
  4. Optional: Route 22 (west of Viridian) has your rival (Lv 8–9), plus Nidoran♂ and Nidoran♀.
  5. Go north through Viridian Forest: mostly Weedle and Kakuna, with Pikachu, Caterpie and Metapod at 5% each.
  6. Brock (Rock). Water, Grass and Fighting all work, and Rock/Ground takes 4× from Water and Grass.
02

Mt. Moon to Cerulean City

Lv 20–22
  1. Route 3 has lots of trainers and Jigglypuff and Spearow. The Pokémon Center at Mt. Moon's entrance sells a Magikarp for $500; it becomes Gyarados at Lv 20.
  2. Mt. Moon: Zubat everywhere, plus Clefairy (6%). At the end you choose the Dome Fossil (Kabuto) or Helix Fossil (Omanyte). Cinnabar's lab revives it later.
  3. In Cerulean, your rival waits on the bridge north of town (Lv 15–18). Then cross Nugget Bridge (five trainers, then a Rocket who gives a Nugget) to Route 25 and Bill's house. Help Bill and you get the S.S. Ticket.
  4. Misty (Water): Grass or Electric. Her Starmie at Lv 21 is the real test.
03

Vermilion City and the S.S. Anne

Lv 24–26
  1. Head south through Routes 5–6 (the Underground Path skips Saffron, which is closed for now). The Pokémon Fan Club chairman in Vermilion gives you a Bike Voucher; trade it at Cerulean's bike shop.
  2. Board the S.S. Anne. Your rival is on board (Lv 16–20). The seasick captain gives you HM01 Cut.
  3. Cut the tree by the gym. Lt. Surge (Electric): Ground is immune. Catch a Diglett in Diglett's Cave (east of Vermilion, via Route 11) and he's easy.
  4. With 10+ Pokémon owned, Oak's aide on Route 2 gives HM05 Flash (you need it for Rock Tunnel).
04

Rock Tunnel, Lavender and Celadon

Lv 30–32
  1. Cut through to Route 9, then go down Route 10 into Rock Tunnel (use Flash) to Lavender Town.
  2. Take Route 8 and the Underground Path west to Celadon City. Upstairs in the department store sells TMs and evolution stones. Celadon Mansion: take the back entrance and climb to the rooftop room for a free Eevee (Lv 25).
  3. Erika (Grass): Fire, Flying, Ice or Psychic. Two of her three are Grass/Poison, so skip Poison moves.
  4. The poster in the Game Corner hides the Rocket Hideout. At the bottom, beat Giovanni for the Silph Scope.
05

Pokémon Tower to Fuchsia City

Lv 38–40
  1. With the Silph Scope, climb Lavender's Pokémon Tower. You fight your rival (Lv 20–25), see through the ghost Marowak and rescue Mr. Fuji, who gives you the Poké Flute.
  2. Wake the Snorlax blocking Route 12 (or Route 16) with the flute. It's Lv 30, and there are two, so both can be caught.
  3. The house on Route 12 gives the Super Rod. On Route 16, Cut behind the Snorlax to reach a house with HM02 Fly.
  4. Take Cycling Road (Route 17) or the Route 12–15 coast to Fuchsia. The Safari Zone's far end has HM03 Surf, and the Gold Teeth. Return the Gold Teeth to the Warden for HM04 Strength. Chansey, Kangaskhan, Scyther, Pinsir and Tauros live in the Safari Zone.
  5. Koga (Poison): Psychic and Ground. Watch for Weezing's Selfdestruct. There's no Levitate in Gen 1, so Earthquake hits Koffing and Weezing.
06

Saffron City and Silph Co.

Lv 42–44
  1. The thirsty Saffron guards want a drink. Buy one from the vending machines on the Celadon store's rooftop.
  2. Silph Co. is a teleporter maze with 11 floors. On 7F an employee gives you Lapras (Lv 15), and your rival ambushes you (Lv 35–40). Beat Giovanni on 11F; the president gives you the Master Ball.
  3. The Fighting Dojo next to the gym gives Hitmonlee or Hitmonchan after you beat the Karate Master.
  4. Sabrina (Psychic) is Gen 1's strongest type. Ghost does nothing to Psychic here and Bug moves are weak. Bring a hard hitter with high Special or Attack (Snorlax, Jolteon, Tauros) and your own Psychic-type.
07

Cinnabar Island

Lv 47–50
  1. Surf south from Pallet (Route 21) or from Fuchsia (Routes 19–20, past the Seafoam Islands, home to Articuno).
  2. The Pokémon Mansion holds the Secret Key to the gym. Its statues flip the switch doors.
  3. The lab revives your fossil (and the Old Amber from Pewter's museum, which becomes Aerodactyl).
  4. Blaine (Fire): Water, Ground or Rock. His Arcanine at Lv 47 is fast; lead with something bulky.
  5. Side trip: Surf Route 10 north of Rock Tunnel to the Power Plant, where Zapdos waits. The Poké Balls there are Voltorb and Electrode traps.
08

Viridian Gym and the Pokémon League

Lv 55–60
  1. Giovanni (Ground) at Viridian Gym: Water, Grass or Ice. Every one of his team is Ground-based.
  2. Route 22 has your rival again (Lv 45–53). Route 23 checks all eight badges, and Victory Road needs Strength for boulder puzzles. Moltres is inside.
  3. At Indigo Plateau, stock up (Full Restores, Revives, Max Potion) before the Elite Four. There's no healing between rooms.
  4. After the Hall of Fame: Cerulean Cave opens, with Mewtwo (Lv 70) at the bottom. Save before you touch it. The Master Ball makes it certain.

It plays like 1996

Gen 1 rules worth knowing

Claude Red keeps the original's battle math, quirks included. These change how you build a team.

Psychic is king

Ghost moves do nothing to Psychic (the original's bug), and Bug moves are few and weak. Your best answers to Alakazam and friends are strong Normal or Psychic attackers with high Special.

One Special stat

Special covers both special attack and special defense, so Amnesia doubles both at once. Types decide physical vs special: Fire, Water, Grass, Electric, Ice, Psychic and Dragon moves are special.

Critical hits come from Speed

Crit chance is based on your Pokémon's base Speed, and Slash, Karate Chop, Razor Leaf and Crabhammer crit about 8× as often. Fast Pokémon like Persian and Jolteon crit a lot.

Odd matchups

Bug and Poison are super-effective on each other. Ice is only neutral against Fire. Keep this in mind against Koga and Erika.

Rebuilt from the original code

Glitches

Claude Red recreates the famous Red and Blue glitches from how the original game actually worked. They're safe here: nothing corrupts your save file.

MissingNo. and the old man

  1. After you have the Pokédex, talk to the old man in Viridian City and watch his catching demo.
  2. Fly straight to Cinnabar Island. Visiting any map with wild grass encounters first overwrites the effect.
  3. Surf up and down the east coast of Cinnabar, on the water tiles right against the shoreline.
  4. Wild Pokémon there are read from the letters of your name, so different names give different encounters. Some letters give real high-level Pokémon; others give MISSINGNO. or 'M.

Meeting MissingNo. adds 128 to the 6th item in your bag, the classic way to duplicate Rare Candies or Master Balls. Put the item you want in slot 6 first.

Side effect: the Hall of Fame screen shows scrambled records afterwards, just like the original. It's only a display effect; your save itself is untouched.

The Mew trick (Trainer-Fly)

  1. Walk into the sight line of a trainer you haven't fought yet, and press START mid-step just as they spot you, so the menu opens before the battle. The classic setup uses a trainer on Route 8, next to Lavender Town.
  2. From that open menu, leave with Fly (or Teleport, Dig or an Escape Rope). Fly to Cerulean City. Until your next trainer battle, the START menu won't open and people won't talk to you.
  3. Walk to Route 25 and let the Youngster with the Lv 17 Slowpoke spot you (you must not have fought him yet). Beat Slowpoke without lowering its Attack. Its Special stat is 21, which is Mew's internal number, and its unchanged Attack stage (7) becomes the level.
  4. Your menu works again. Fly to Lavender and walk back onto the route you escaped from. The START menu opens by itself. Close it and a Lv 7 Mew battle starts.

Another opponent's leftover stats give other Pokémon: the last enemy's Special picks the species and its Attack stage sets the level.

Cable Club, any Pokémon Center

Social Zone and extras

The Cable Club receptionist in every Pokémon Center takes you to the Social Zone.

Battle Tower

  • Pick 3 Pokémon. They're healed and set to Lv 50 for every fight. There are no items and no EXP.
  • Each win earns 1 BP, and every 7th win in a row adds 3 more. One loss resets the streak.
  • Opponents are built from strong level-up and TM moves and never use legendaries. Sleep and paralysis are your best friends.

Friends, trades and versus

  • Link battles: share your team as a link, and your friend battles it. No server is involved.
  • Trades by link: send an offer, your friend answers, and both sides get the Pokémon. Trade evolutions (Kadabra, Haunter, Machoke, Graveler) evolve this way.
  • Local versus: 2 players on one device.

Who's That Pokémon?

On the title screen: name the silhouette before time runs out. DAILY gives everyone the same 10 each day; ENDLESS runs until you miss.

Share and customize

A new game lets you design your trainer's look. SHARE in the START menu makes cards for badges, catches and Pokédex milestones.
